# Arlo — 4 "Banger" Scripts (2 TikTok, 2 Reels)

Written for a brand-new account with zero followers. That means the algorithm
is the only audience that matters, and it only cares about one thing: does
someone watch this to the end and not scroll. Every script here is built
around that, not around looking professional.

**The 4 rules used to write these:**
1. **Hook in the first 0.5-1 second, no intro.** No "hey guys," no logo, no
   "so I built a company." First frame = the weirdest, most specific moment.
2. **An open loop, not a statement.** "This AI answered my phone" is a fact
   (boring). "I called my own business and forgot I was talking to a robot"
   is a mystery (you have to watch to resolve it).
3. **Native to the platform, not an ad.** Shot like a real moment caught on
   a phone, not produced. The worse the lighting looks, the more it reads
   as real — real is what performs from a 2-day-old account.
4. **The comment section is part of the script.** Every one of these ends on
   something people argue about or ask about — that's what turns a view
   into a share/comment, which is what actually gets the algorithm to push
   it past your own following.

## TIKTOK #1 — "I didn't realise for 40 seconds"

**Format:** screen recording of a real phone call (you calling +1 608 946
0425), face-cam reaction inset bottom corner.

**0:00 (first frame, before you've said anything):** On-screen text, big,
already on screen when the video starts:
> **"I called my own business and didn't clock it wasn't a real person for
> 40 seconds"**

**0:00-0:03:** No talking yet. Just the phone screen, call connecting, ringing.

**0:03-0:25:** Play real Angel call audio — pick a stretch where she handles
an interruption or answers something specific naturally. Let it breathe, no
voiceover talking over it. Trust the audio.

**0:25 (the turn):** Freeze the video. On-screen text stamps over the frame:
> **"That's not a person. That's my AI receptionist."**

**0:25-0:35:** Face cam, straight into lens, half-laughing:
*"I run this company. I know it's AI. And I still forgot for a minute.
Imagine your actual customers."*

**End card, on screen, no voiceover:**
> **"Comment 'CALL ME' and I'll have her ring you. Not kidding."**

**Caption:** i forgot my own AI wasn't a real person 💀 comment CALL ME and
she'll actually ring you #ai #smallbusiness #fyp

**Why this is the one to post first:** the hook is a confession, not a
pitch — nobody skips a "I got fooled" story. The CTA is a dare, which is
what actually drives comments from strangers (they want to test you).

## TIKTOK #2 — "The £3,000 sentence"

**Format:** straight to camera, tight crop, no B-roll. Delivered fast, like
you're mid-thought, not reading a script.

**0:00 (first word, no lead-in):**
*"A missed phone call just cost a small business three grand. Let me show
you the maths, because nobody ever shows you this."*

**0:03-0:15:** Hold up fingers / write on a notepad in frame as you say it
(visual counting reads as more "real" than a slide):
*"Ten missed calls a week. Average job's a hundred and fifty quid. That's
three thousand pounds a month — gone. To whoever picks up the phone next."*

**0:15-0:22 (the twist):**
*"And here's the bit that messes with me — the business owner never even
sees it happen. You only see the calls that get through. The three grand
just... vanishes, silently, every single month."*

**0:22-0:30 (the payoff / soft reveal):**
*"So I built something that answers every single one of those calls instead.
It's called Arlo. It's AI. It sounds like this—"* [cut straight into 3-4
seconds of real Angel call audio, then hard cut to black]

**End card text only:**
> **"£3,000 a month. Or one phone call. Comment ARLO."**

**Caption:** the money math nobody shows small business owners 📉 #smallbusiness #ai #buildinpublic

**Why this works:** money-shock hooks are one of the most reliable viral
templates because the "wait, WHAT" reaction is instant and universal — you
don't need to run a business to feel that number. The AI reveal rides in
on the back of the shock instead of leading with it.

## REEL #1 — "3 phones ring at the same time"

**Format:** split-screen / quick-cut triptych. Needs light editing (CapCut,
15 min) but no acting beyond you narrating over B-roll you can shoot in an
afternoon (your own phone screen, a clock, a "missed call" notification).

**0:00 (cold open, on-screen text already up):**
> **"3 small businesses. Same missed call. 3 different endings."**

**0:00-0:07 — Business 1 (no answer):** Phone ringing, ringing, ringing,
goes to voicemail beep. On-screen text: *"73% of people who hit voicemail
never call back."* Cut to black.

**0:07-0:14 — Business 2 (answered late):** Phone ringing, you shown
rushing, breathless "hello— sorry— who's this—" caller already hung up.
On-screen text: *"Too slow is the same as never."*

**0:14-0:25 — Business 3 (Arlo):** Real Angel call audio playing, answered
within 2 seconds, naturally handling the caller. On-screen text appears
mid-clip: *"This one's AI. Answered in 2 seconds. Every time."*

**0:25-0:30 (you, to camera, close-up):**
*"Same missed call. Only one of these businesses keeps the job."*

**End card:** **"This is Arlo. Follow for the build."**

**Caption:** same phone call, 3 different businesses, only 1 keeps the
customer 📞 #smallbusiness #buildinpublic #ai

**Why this works on Reels specifically:** Instagram's algorithm (and
audience) rewards a clear "before/after/comparison" structure more than raw
shock — it's shareable to a business-owner friend with "this is you 😭"
energy, which is the exact share trigger Reels needs to travel outside your
followers.

## REEL #2 — "I let strangers test my AI live"

**Format:** documentary/vlog style, real screen recordings stitched fast.
This is the highest-ceiling one if it hits, because it's inherently
rewatchable and comment-bait built in.

**0:00 (cold open, text already on screen, you mid-action):**
> **"I posted my AI's phone number and told strangers to try and break it"**

**0:00-0:05:** Quick flash of a comments screen (blurred/anonymised) full of
questions and dares people left.

**0:05-0:22:** Three rapid-fire clips (5-6 sec each), hard-cut, no
transitions, real Angel call audio each time:
- A weird/off-topic question she deflects well, on-screen text: *"attempt 1: failed to break her"*
- An objection/price push she handles, text: *"attempt 2: failed"*
- Something genuinely funny or human she says, text: *"attempt 3: I gave up"*

**0:22-0:28 (you, to camera):**
*"Three days, dozens of calls, nobody's broken her yet. I don't think
they're going to."*

**End card:** **"Try it yourself — comment your hardest question."**

**Caption:** I dared strangers to break my AI receptionist. Day 3: still
undefeated 🤖 #buildinpublic #ai #smallbusiness

**Why this works:** it converts your audience into unpaid QA testers and
turns every comment into free future content — it's a format that compounds
across a whole week of posts rather than being a one-off.

## Filming priority
Post **TikTok #1** first — it needs zero editing beyond a text overlay and
uses a real call you can record today. It's also the lowest-risk: if the
call goes slightly imperfect, that only makes the "I forgot it wasn't real"
hook more believable, not less.
